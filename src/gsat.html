Why GSAT?

The Problem

Environmental pollutants — pharmaceuticals, pesticides, industrial chemicals — rarely remain in their original form. Microbial communities and chemical processes break them down into transformation products (TPs), which are often more persistent and more toxic than the parent compound.

Traditional toxicokinetic simulations can take hours per compound and cannot scale to assess thousands of TPs across multi-generational pathways. Regulatory risk assessment is severely bottlenecked.

GSAT addresses this by learning both molecular structure (graph) and reaction sequence context (transformer) simultaneously — enabling near-instant, high-accuracy toxicity prediction across full metabolic trees.

Scale of the Challenge

  • 12 priority pharmaceutical & pesticide pollutants studied
  • 4.7 million reaction steps traced across multi-generational biotransformation trees
  • 68.2% of metabolic TPs show increased toxicity vs. parent compound
  • Average toxicity amplification: 15.4-fold
  • Some TPs exhibit up to 400× higher EC₅₀ toxicity
  • Traditional simulation: ~6–12 hours per compound; GSAT: <5 seconds
Model Architecture

How GSAT Works

GSAT fuses two complementary views of a molecule: its graph topology (atoms as nodes, bonds as edges) and its SMILES sequence representation (chemical grammar). A cross-modal attention layer learns which structural features drive toxicity.

Graph Attention Network (GAT)

Each atom is a node with RDKit-derived features: atomic number, hybridization, formal charge, ring membership, aromaticity, and 3D ETKDG coordinates. Four GAT layers with multi-head attention aggregate neighborhood information, learning which atoms and bonds matter most for biotransformation reactivity.

Sequence Transformer

The SMILES string is tokenized at the character level and processed through a 4-layer, 8-head Transformer encoder. Positional encodings allow the model to learn chemical grammar patterns — ring closure notation, branch points, stereochemistry — that the graph representation cannot directly encode as sequence.

Cross-Modal Attention Bridge

A novel cross-attention layer allows graph node embeddings to attend to sequence token embeddings and vice versa. This bidirectional coupling lets the model resolve ambiguities — e.g., which aromatic carbon in the graph corresponds to which token position — producing richer, more informative joint representations.

Atom-level attention heatmap (example: Ciprofloxacin TP-1 · assumed visualization)

Brighter nodes = higher cross-modal attention weight. The model learns that hydroxylated aromatic carbons and quinolone nitrogen drive toxicity prediction.

Training Strategy

Handling Imbalanced Toxicity Data

Focal Loss

Standard MSE loss underweights rare high-toxicity TPs — the most dangerous compounds for environmental risk. GSAT uses focal-style regression loss that up-weights hard samples, forcing the model to accurately predict compounds at the tails of the toxicity distribution (EC₅₀ < 0.1 mg/L).

SMILES Augmentation

Each molecule has multiple valid SMILES representations (canonical vs. randomized). During training, 8–12 augmented SMILES are generated per molecule. This forces the sequence encoder to learn atom-graph invariant features rather than canonical string artifacts, improving generalization by ~4% R².

Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA)

Rather than using a single endpoint model, SWA averages weights across the final 30 training epochs. This flattens the loss landscape and improves generalization, reducing overfitting on smaller pollutant-class clusters — critical when some compound classes have <50 training examples.

Key Hyperparameters

  • GAT layers: 4 · Hidden dim: 256
  • Transformer heads: 8 · Layers: 4
  • Cross-attention heads: 4
  • Dropout: 0.3 · LR: 3×10⁻⁴ (cosine decay)
  • Batch size: 64 · Epochs: 200 + SWA 30
  • Feature set: 200+ descriptors including 3D ETKDG
Extension: Pathway Prediction

Graph Transformer for Reaction Class Prediction

Beyond toxicity, the GSAT framework was extended to predict which biotransformation reaction a molecule will undergo — enabling automated construction of full metabolic trees.

Future Work

Where GSAT Goes Next

3D Geometric Extension

Incorporating 3D molecular conformations (ETKDG + SE(3)-equivariant layers) to capture steric effects that govern enzyme binding and biotransformation selectivity.

Mixture Toxicity

Extending from single-compound to mixture toxicity prediction — modeling synergistic and antagonistic interactions between multiple TPs co-occurring in environmental samples.

Online Web Tool

A public Gradio-based web interface allowing researchers to input any SMILES and receive instant biotransformation pathway trees with per-node toxicity scores and attention maps.

Pretraining on Environmental DB

Self-supervised pretraining on 2M+ compounds from PubChem Environmental Subset + enviPath to build a foundation model specialized for environmental fate and toxicity.

Uncertainty Quantification

Bayesian dropout and conformal prediction to provide calibrated uncertainty intervals — essential for regulatory decision-making where false negatives carry environmental risk.

Multi-endpoint Prediction

Jointly predicting EC₅₀, LC₅₀, IGC₅₀, and persistence (half-life) in a multi-task head — learning shared molecular features across all ecotoxicity endpoints simultaneously.
