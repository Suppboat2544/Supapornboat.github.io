Part I

CRAM Generative Model

AI-Driven Generation of Carboxyl-Rich Aliphatic/Alicyclic Molecules — A Property-Conditioned Deep Learning Approach for Understanding Refractory Dissolved Organic Matter

The Challenge

Marine dissolved organic matter (DOM) is Earth's largest reservoir of reduced organic carbon — yet its molecular structure remains largely unknown. Deep-ocean DOM is dominated by carboxyl-rich aliphatic/alicyclic molecules (CRAMs), but for any given molecular formula from HR-MS data, PubChem returns only 1–2 candidate structures — far too few to capture true chemical diversity.

Traditional databases were built for pharmaceuticals, not environmental chemistry. The chemical space of marine DOM is essentially uncharted.

The CRAM Approach

  • Curated a CRAM-specific training corpus from marine DOM databases and computational screening
  • Built a property-conditioned generative model: VAE encoder with Graph Attention Networks (GAT) + GRU-SELFIES decoder
  • Model conditions on molecular properties: double bond equivalence (DBE), O/C ratio, H/C ratio — key CRAM descriptors
  • Integrated with a chemical space refinement pipeline using HR-MS/MS spectral matching and graph-based filtering
  • Result: Median candidate isomers per deep-ocean molecular formula increased from 1.4 → 9.3

Key Results

  • Median candidate isomers per marine molecular formula: 1.4 (PubChem) → 9.3 (CRAM model)
  • Generated molecules satisfy key CRAM property constraints (O/C, H/C, DBE ranges)
  • Chemical space refinement pipeline integrates HR-MS/MS spectral matching for experimental validation
  • Network analysis reveals structural clustering patterns consistent with known DOM composition

Significance

  • First property-conditioned generative model specifically targeting marine CRAM chemistry
  • Enables de novo hypothesis generation for unknown DOM structures
  • Bridges the gap between high-resolution mass spectrometry data and structural annotation
  • Opens pathways for studying marine carbon cycling at molecular resolution
Part II

CRAM-GTransformer

Bidirectional Graph Transformer for Site-Specific and Pathway-Level CRAM Biotransformation — predicting how refractory DOM molecules transform in aquatic environments.

Why Biotransformation Matters

CRAM molecules in DOM are not static — they undergo microbial and photochemical biotransformation that alters their structure, reactivity, and environmental fate. Understanding which atoms react, at which sites, and along which pathways is essential for modeling marine carbon cycling.

Existing tools predict reactions at the compound level but cannot resolve site-specific atom transformations — exactly the information needed to trace CRAM degradation pathways.

CRAM-GTransformer Design

  • Bidirectional Graph Transformer — attends over both reactant and product molecular graphs simultaneously
  • Trained on 210K+ curated reaction pairs across 99 biotransformation classes
  • Predicts both the reaction class and the specific reactive site within the molecule
  • Training tricks: Focal Loss for class imbalance + SMILES augmentation + Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA)
  • Achieves 87% Top-3 accuracy across all biotransformation classes
