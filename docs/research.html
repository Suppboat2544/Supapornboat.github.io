DOM Transformation

Dissolved Organic Matter (DOM) in aquatic environments is one of the most chemically complex natural mixtures on Earth. My work focuses on constructing a chemical space refinement framework to decode its structural complexity using high-resolution mass spectrometry (HR-MS/MS).

  • Graph-based filtering, network analysis, and spectral matching of HR-MS/MS data
  • Property-conditioned generative model (CRAM): VAE + GAT encoder + GRU-SELFIES decoder
  • Increased median candidate isomers per deep-ocean molecular formula from 1.4 → 9.3
  • ChemRxiv preprint: doi:10.26434/chemrxiv-2025-9s13m-v2

Collaboration interest: Environmental chemistry, HR-MS/MS data sharing, marine carbon cycling, generative chemistry.

Biotransformation Modeling

Environmental pollutants rarely remain in their original form — they undergo microbial and chemical biotransformation, often producing metabolites more toxic than the parent compound.

  • Published GSAT model (ACS ES&T Water): R²=0.907, 98.9% faster than traditional methods
  • Analyzed 4.7 million reaction steps across 12 priority pollutants
  • 68.2% of metabolic transformations increase toxicity by an average 15.4-fold
  • Graph Transformer for Pathway Prediction: 210K+ reaction pairs, 99 classes, 87% Top-3 accuracy
  • Techniques: Focal Loss, SMILES augmentation, Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA)

Collaboration interest: Environmental toxicology, metabolomics, biodegradation databases, regulatory risk assessment.

MS1 Formula Assignment

Non-target screening of complex environmental samples generates vast numbers of molecular signals — accurately assigning molecular formulas and identifying unknown compounds is a major bottleneck.

  • Computational pipelines for accurate molecular formula assignment from HR-MS1 data
  • Mass difference network algorithms for automated PFAS transformation product identification
  • High-throughput profiling integrating spectral matching and chemical space refinement
  • Applied to marine DOM, freshwater, and PFAS-contaminated samples

Collaboration interest: Environmental analytical chemistry, non-target screening datasets, PFAS research, water quality monitoring.

Kinetic Deep Learning

Predicting how fast chemicals degrade in the environment — and under what conditions — is essential for risk assessment and water quality management.

  • Hybrid GAT + MPNN architecture for molecular half-life prediction: R²=0.847
  • 200+ molecular descriptors including 3D conformational features (ETKDG)
  • SHAP & Integrated Gradients for model interpretability; Gradio web interface
  • Physics-Informed Neural Networks: R²=0.93 for degradation across 0–6000m ocean depth
  • Discovered 17,500× half-life variation from surface to abyssal waters
  • Validated against ARGO & GLODAP oceanographic databases (300K+ measurements)

Collaboration interest: Oceanography, environmental fate modeling, kinetic databases, physics-informed ML.

Clustering in High-Dimensional Datasets

Molecular and biological datasets are inherently high-dimensional. Standard Euclidean-based clustering methods often fail — requiring specialized approaches tailored to sparse, heterogeneous feature spaces.

  • Hierarchical DBSCAN for density-based clustering of large molecular datasets
  • UMAP-assisted dimensionality reduction prior to clustering
  • Graph-partitioning methods for chemical space network segmentation
  • Applied to 1.29M marine enzyme sequences (ESM-2 embeddings + k-mer features)
  • Chemical space network clustering for DOM formula grouping
  • Investigating optimal distance metrics for molecular fingerprint spaces

Collaboration interest: Large-scale omics datasets, metabolomics, chemical library clustering, bioinformatics pipelines.

Marine Enzyme Discovery

The ocean harbors vast untapped enzymatic diversity relevant to carbon cycling, bioremediation, and biotechnology. Computational discovery at scale is essential.

  • ESM-2 protein language model embeddings + k-mer sequence features
  • Hierarchical DBSCAN clustering of 1.29M marine enzyme sequences
  • Structural and kinetic analysis of carboxyl-metabolizing enzymes (FPocket, MD simulations)
  • Universal scaling laws for enzyme kinetics: R²=0.91
  • Preprint: "Structural and Kinetic Analysis of Carboxyl-Metabolizing Enzymes" (ChemRxiv)

Collaboration interest: Marine microbiology, enzyme databases, protein structure prediction, blue carbon research.

Generative Molecular Models

Generative models allow us to explore chemical spaces beyond known compounds — proposing novel molecules with desired environmental or pharmaceutical properties.

  • CRAM: property-conditioned VAE + GAT encoder + GRU-SELFIES decoder
  • Conditioned on physicochemical properties (MW, DBE, O/C ratio, H/C ratio)
  • Specifically designed for marine DOM chemical space exploration
  • Enhanced candidate isomers per deep-ocean formula: 1.4 → 9.3×
  • Applications: de novo molecule generation for environmental and drug discovery contexts

Collaboration interest: Drug discovery, materials science, environmental chemistry, molecular generation benchmarking.

PFAS & Emerging Contaminants

Per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) are persistent pollutants of global concern. Identifying their transformation products and understanding their fate is critical for water safety.

  • Mass difference network algorithm for automated transformation product identification
  • High-throughput non-target screening of PFAS in water samples
  • Published in Water Research: doi:10.1016/j.watres.2025.123645 — 13 citations
  • Integration with GNN-based toxicity prediction for risk prioritization
  • JST-funded research collaboration with LLNL (2024–2025)

Collaboration interest: PFAS monitoring, water treatment, non-target analysis, regulatory toxicology.
